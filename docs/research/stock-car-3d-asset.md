## 1. Exporting from iRacing — no; templates — yes (2D only)

The current EULA (dated 2026-08-05, [PDF](https://ir-core-sites.iracing.com/members/pdfs/20260805-iRacing_Terms_of_Use_and_EULA_dated_Aug_05_2026.pdf), linked from [iracing.com/terms-use-eula](https://www.iracing.com/terms-use-eula/)) defines "Content" as "additional downloadable content such as tracks and cars" (p.1), states in §4.1 that "the Content (including ... cars, objects ... artwork ...) are owned by iRacing or its licensors" and "the Sim Client and the Content are licensed to you; you will not obtain any ownership interest", and in §6.1(c) forbids you to "translate, reverse engineer, derive source code from, modify, disassemble, decompile, or create derivative works based on ... the Content". §6.2 reserves derivative works exclusively to iRacing; §6.3 bars any "commercial purpose". So ripping the Mustang mesh out of the sim is a contract breach on top of a copyright problem, and there is no official export. Two further clauses matter for the livery: §8.1(b) says all Materials you submit to the Service (explicitly including car paints) "will be the property of iRacing" via an irrevocable assignment, and §8.1(e) says you will not include third-party trademarks/logos in a paint scheme "or on any website" without written permission. The Goodyear, 3M and Ford marks visible in `jimmy_car.png` are iRacing-stamped sponsor decals, not yours to reproduce.

What _is_ available: PSD paint templates for every car, "created, provided, and hosted by iRacing" ([Trading Paints template index](https://www.tradingpaints.com/cartemplates), which lists the NASCAR Cup Next Gen Ford Mustang among others; iRacing's own zip links are on [support.iracing.com](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000157675-templates-to-all-iracing-cars-helmet-and-suits)). iRacing's [painting guide](https://members.iracing.com/pdfs/2015_01_20_custom_paint_schemes.pdf) says the car textures are "1024 x 1024 or 2048 x 2048 pixels" TGA, with layers marking where iRacing stamps numbers and sponsors. The template is the UV layout of _iRacing's_ mesh, so it cannot be dropped onto a different mesh; but the user's own paint PSD/TGA in `Documents\iRacing\paint\<car>\car_<id>.tga` is a 2048² source for the #45 artwork — vastly better than the 416×175 PNG, and (trademark decals aside) authored by him.

## 2. Licences for third-party models

Sketchfab lets uploaders pick a CC licence for downloadable models, with CC BY the default ([Sketchfab blog](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/an-introduction-to-creative-commons-licenses/)). CC BY 4.0 permits any use including commercial if you keep creator identification, a licence notice and "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material" (§3(a)(1), [legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode)); Sketchfab's own [download guidelines](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/guidelines) want creator name, model link and licence shown wherever the asset is used. CC BY-NC's test is whether use is "primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation" ([BY-NC 4.0 §1](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode.en)); a hire-me portfolio is a grey area, so prefer CC BY/CC0. The Sketchfab/Fab **Standard** licence forbids making the material available "as a stand-alone file (or group of files)" ([sketchfab.com/licenses](https://sketchfab.com/licenses)) — a GLB in `public/` is exactly that. Paid stock is worse: TurboSquid requires models be "contained in proprietary formats so that they cannot be opened or imported in a publicly available software application", with a WebGL carve-out only for Unity/Unreal/Lumberyard/Stingray exports ([TurboSquid licence](https://blog.turbosquid.com/royalty-free-license/)); CGTrader requires you to "take all commercially reasonable measures to prevent the end user from gaining access to the Product" ([CGTrader terms](https://www.cgtrader.com/pages/terms-and-conditions)). A fetchable GLB fails both. Provenance caveat: a CC label is only as good as the uploader's right to grant it, and many Sketchfab NASCAR models are game rips (the duplicate Mustang uploads above are the pattern). Before shipping, check the author's profile for original work, and consider messaging them.

## 3. Livery options from the PNG

`jimmy_car.png` is 416×175 RGBA, the car fills the frame (opaque bbox 0,2–416,175), one front-three-quarter view: ~80 px per metre of car, the "45" about 60 px tall, "beta ui" barely legible, and the right side, roof, hood and rear unseen. iRacing's texture is 2048², so the PNG has ~1/25 of the texel density and covers half the body. (a) **Projecting the PNG onto a UV-mapped mesh** is not viable for hero quality — blurry, one-sided, and the mesh's UVs won't match anyway; only usable as a blurry colour hint. (b) **Flat body colour + decals** is the strong option: sample the blue, rebuild "45" and "beta ui" as SVG text on a 512²–1024² decal atlas (single WebP, 20–60 KB), place quads on doors/roof, drop the third-party sponsor marks. If the 2048² paint TGA can be found, bake it onto the replacement mesh's UVs in Blender instead. (c) **Point cloud** sidesteps texture fidelity entirely; Remix's `racecar.pts` carries no colours at all (flag word is 0) and reads as a glowing silhouette. A blue tint with a handful of white points where the number sits is about the limit of what the PNG supports; it's silhouette quality by construction.

## 4. Compression pipeline and realistic sizes

Commands (from the CLI [source](https://github.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/blob/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts) / [docs](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli) and the [gltfpack README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/gltf/README.md)):

```
gltf-transform optimize in.glb out.glb --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --texture-size 1024 \
  --simplify-ratio 0.25 --simplify-error 0.001          # --compress draco|meshopt|quantize; --texture-compress ktx2|webp|avif
gltf-transform etc1s in.glb out.glb   /  gltf-transform uastc in.glb out.glb   # KTX2; needs KTX-Software's `ktx` on PATH
gltfpack -i in.glb -o out.glb -cc -si 0.25 -tc            # -tc/-tw need the native binary; npm build refuses WebP/KTX2
```

Draco vs meshopt, per gltf-transform's extension docs: Draco "can reduce filesize by ~95%" when geometry exceeds ~1 MB, but "for geometry <1MB, the size of the WASM decoder library may outweigh size savings" ([KHRDracoMeshCompression](https://gltf-transform.dev/modules/extensions/classes/KHRDracoMeshCompression)); meshopt "offers a lightweight decoder with very fast runtime decompression" but "for the full benefits ... apply gzip, brotli" — "without this secondary compression, the size reduction is considerably less" ([EXTMeshoptCompression](https://gltf-transform.dev/modules/extensions/classes/EXTMeshoptCompression); same point in the [EXT_meshopt_compression spec](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Vendor/EXT_meshopt_compression/README.md)). Decoder cost in three.js `dev` ([tree](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/examples/jsm/libs)): `meshopt_decoder.module.js` 29 KB; Draco `gltf/draco_decoder.wasm` 192 KB + 58 KB wrapper; `basis_transcoder.wasm` 527 KB + 57 KB for KTX2. KTX2 saves GPU memory (Khronos' [KTX artist guide](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/blob/main/KTXArtistGuide.md): Duck 1.5 MB → 277 KB VRAM at the same file size), not bytes on the wire — for one car texture the 580 KB transcoder isn't worth it; WebP is. GLTFLoader wires all three via `setMeshoptDecoder`/`setDRACOLoader`/`setKTX2Loader` ([GLTFLoader.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js)).

Measured on ToyCar (108,936 tris, 5.42 MB; gzip -9 sizes in parentheses): meshopt + WebP@2048 → 859 KB (701); Draco + WebP@2048 → 563 KB (545); quantize only → 1.94 MB; **simplify 0.2 (→23.5k tris) + WebP@512 + meshopt → 375 KB (325); + Draco → 256 KB (249)**; same with textures shrunk to 32 px → 253 KB (203), i.e. ~200 KB of geometry for 23.5k tris with normals+UVs. Kenney truck (946 tris, 512² colormap): 92 KB → 14.7 KB meshopt+WebP (9.5 gz) or 8.4 KB Draco (7.4 gz). So: **hero tier** (20–40k tris, one 1024² WebP body atlas, flat materials) lands at roughly 250–400 KB; **silhouette tier** (2–8k tris, no textures) at 20–60 KB. Draco wins by ~25% at the same triangle count but costs 250 KB of decoder, which erases the gain until you ship several hero-size assets; meshopt's gain assumes the CDN brotli/gzips `model/gltf-binary` — verify Netlify's response headers on a `.glb`, or pre-compress.

## 5. Point cloud vs compressed GLB

Remix's format ([model-loader.ts](https://github.com/remix-run/remix-website/blob/main/app/actions/public/remix-landing/engine/model-loader.ts)): 36-byte `PTS1` header, then int16 xyz per point (6 B), optional uint8 RGB. `public/landing/models/racecar.pts` is 600,036 bytes = 100,000 points, colour flag 0; gzip -9 leaves it at 597 KB — quantized random-order points don't compress. Measured alternatives for 100k points sampled from ToyCar: raw glTF POINTS with RGB 1.6 MB; `gltfpack -cc` 466 KB (405 gz); `gltf-transform meshopt` 920 KB. At 30k points: PTS1 180 KB, gltfpack -cc 158 KB (142 gz). Draco also encodes point clouds ([README](https://github.com/google/draco/blob/main/README.md): "much better compression rates for larger and denser point clouds") but glTF's Draco extension covers triangles only, so it would be a bespoke `.drc` fetch. Trade-off: a point cloud costs ~6 B/point floor and needs ~100k points to read as a solid car at hero distance — 600 KB, more than a 23.5k-tri hero mesh (~250–325 KB) and 10× a silhouette mesh; below ~30k points it becomes sparse dust. It buys a distinctive glowing look and immunity to livery fidelity, not bytes. If a point look is wanted, bake points from the _compressed_ mesh at runtime (`MeshSurfaceSampler` in three.js) and ship only the mesh — the 100k points cost nothing on the wire.

## Recommendation

**Asset:** 2017 NASCAR Chevrolet SS (CC BY, 59k faces) or Car of Tomorrow (CC BY, 27k) for the hero tier, after a provenance check on the author; Madco3D's Low Poly Stock Cars (CC BY, ~1.9k faces per car) for the always-loaded silhouette tier. Attribute author + model link + "CC BY 4.0" on a credits page. If it must be a Gen-6 Mustang, the honest path is modelling a ~10k-tri one in Blender (no CC BY Mustang exists; the labelled ones are re-uploads of an NC-SA model). Never rip from iRacing, and don't buy from TurboSquid/CGTrader for a public GLB.

**Livery:** flat blue body material + a 512–1024² WebP decal atlas rebuilt from SVG ("45", "beta ui"), no sponsor marks; upgrade to a baked 2048 → 1024 texture only if the original iRacing paint TGA turns up.

**Pipeline:** Blender: delete interior/engine/underbody, decimate to budget, unify materials, export GLB → `gltf-transform optimize --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --texture-size 1024 --simplify-ratio R` (Draco only if you end up with >1 MB of geometry) → confirm brotli on `.glb` from Netlify → three.js `GLTFLoader` + `MeshoptDecoder` (29 KB) loaded via dynamic import after first paint; silhouette GLB (≤50 KB) by default, hero GLB (≤400 KB) on interaction or `saveData`/connection checks. Sample points from the mesh at runtime if the Remix look is wanted.
